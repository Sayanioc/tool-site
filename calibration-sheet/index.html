What this tool does

It produces a 5-point (0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %) calibration record for a 4–20 mA transmitter: the input to apply at each point, the expected output, the as-found and as-left errors in percent of span, and pass/fail against your tolerance. The whole sheet – including readings – is stored in the page link, so you can bookmark it or send it to a colleague. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Formula

Input applied   = LRV + (URV − LRV) × p / 100
Expected (lin.) = 4 + 16 × p / 100  mA
Expected (sqrt) = 4 + 16 × √(p / 100)  mA
Error % span    = (measured − expected) / 16 × 100
Pass            ⇔ |error| ≤ tolerance

Worked example

A 0–10 bar transmitter, tolerance ±0.5 % of span. At 50 % (5 bar applied) the expected output is 12.000 mA. As-found reading 12.10 mA → error = 0.10 / 16 × 100 = +0.625 % → FAIL. After trimming, as-left 12.01 mA → +0.0625 % → PASS.

Common mistakes

  • Calculating error as % of reading instead of % of span – the tolerance in most site procedures is % of span (calibrated range).
  • Using a reference standard that is not at least 3–4 times more accurate than the tolerance (test uncertainty ratio).
  • Not recording as-found before adjusting – as-found data shows drift and is required for interval analysis.
  • Ignoring hysteresis: for critical loops also test downscale (100 → 0 %).
  • Not stabilising the pressure/temperature before reading, or forgetting the head correction when the calibrator is not at transmitter level.

Standards and good practice

  • ISO/IEC 17025 and ISO 10012 – measurement management and calibration records (traceability, as-found / as-left).
  • IEC 61298-2 – process measurement and control devices, test methods (measured error and hysteresis at points across the span).
  • IEC 61511 – proof testing of safety instrumented functions requires as-found records.
