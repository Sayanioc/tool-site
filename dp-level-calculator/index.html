Step-by-step working

    What this calculator does

    A DP transmitter measures level as hydrostatic head: ΔP = ρ·g·h. When the transmitter is not level with the 0 % point, or when the low-pressure side has a liquid-filled reference (wet leg or capillary), the zero must be shifted. This calculator finds the transmitter LRV and URV for each case.

    Formula

    HP side = SG_liquid × L + SG_HP-leg × e
LP side = 0                    (open tank / dry leg)
        = SG_wet × (d + e)     (wet leg)
        = SG_fill × (d + e)    (remote seals)
LRV = HP(L = h0) − LP     URV = HP(L = h0 + H) − LP     Span = SG_liquid × H
(heads in mm × SG = mmH₂O; 1 mmH₂O = 9.80665 Pa)

    Worked example (wet leg)

    Closed tank, liquid SG 0.8, taps 6000 mm apart, 0 % level 500 mm above the HP tap, span 5000 mm, transmitter 1000 mm below the HP tap, wet leg filled with water (SG 1.0).

    1. HP leg = 0.8 × 1000 = 800 mmH₂O
    2. HP(0 %) = 0.8 × 500 + 800 = 1200; HP(100 %) = 0.8 × 5500 + 800 = 5200 mmH₂O
    3. LP = 1.0 × (6000 + 1000) = 7000 mmH₂O
    4. LRV = 1200 − 7000 = −5800 mmH₂O; URV = 5200 − 7000 = −1800 mmH₂O (span 4000)

    With remote seals on both sides the transmitter elevation cancels out: LRV = SG_liquid × h0 − SG_fill × d, independent of where the transmitter is mounted (but mounting it above the lower seal reduces the vacuum margin).

    Common mistakes

    • Using design SG instead of SG at operating temperature – level error is proportional to the SG error.
    • Forgetting the HP impulse-line head when the transmitter is mounted below the tap.
    • Wet leg partly drained or evaporated (or condensate leg not yet filled on start-up): reading is wrong until the leg is full.
    • Swapping HP and LP: on a wet-leg installation the HP side must connect to the bottom tap.
    • Ignoring the vapour space density for high-pressure gas/steam drums (e.g. boiler drum level needs density compensation).
    • Not using the seal manufacturer's fill-fluid SG at the capillary temperature.

    Limits and standards

    Hydrostatic principle (P = ρgh) as used in ISA and manufacturer ranging guides; units per NIST SP 811 (conventional mmH₂O = 9.80665 Pa). Assumes static liquid, uniform density, negligible vapour density and no temperature effects on the capillaries. Verify against the transmitter manufacturer's ranging procedure and the as-built installation.
