Communication

What this calculator does

A 2-wire transmitter is powered by the same loop it signals on. Every resistance in series – the AI card's sense resistor, intrinsic-safety barrier or isolator, cable and any loop indicator – drops voltage, and what is left at the transmitter terminals must stay above its minimum operating (lift-off) voltage even at the highest current the transmitter can drive. This tool checks that budget.

Formula

R_cable = 2 × L × r / 1000                 (two conductors)
R_total = R_card + R_barrier + R_other + R_cable
R_max   = (V_supply − V_min) / I_max
V_tx    = V_supply − I_max × R_total        must be ≥ V_min
V_supply(min) = V_min + I_max × R_total

Worked example

24 V supply, transmitter minimum 10.5 V, 23 mA maximum, 250 Ω AI card, 500 m of 1.0 mm² cable (2 × 0.5 km × 18.1 Ω/km = 18.1 Ω). R_total = 268.1 Ω. R_max = (24 − 10.5) / 0.023 = 587 Ω, so there is 319 Ω of margin. Voltage at the transmitter at 23 mA = 24 − 0.023 × 268.1 = 17.83 V ≥ 10.5 V → OK.

Common mistakes

  • Checking at 20 mA only: the transmitter must still work when it drives its upscale alarm (≥ 21 mA per NAMUR NE 43, often 22.5–23 mA).
  • Ignoring the IS barrier: a shunt-diode barrier can be 300 Ω or more end-to-end; isolators often specify a voltage drop instead.
  • Using the transmitter's basic lift-off voltage when HART, a local display, or SIL options raise it.
  • Removing the 250 Ω resistor when moving to a low-impedance input: HART needs at least 230 Ω in the loop to communicate.
  • Counting the cable length once – the loop current flows out and back through two conductors.

Standards and references

  • IEC 60381-1 – 4–20 mA analogue signals; NAMUR NE 43 – failure signal levels.
  • HART Communication Foundation physical layer (FSK) – loop resistance 230–1100 Ω.
  • IEC 60228 – conductor resistance of copper cables (max values at 20 °C).
  • Transmitter datasheet load diagram (e.g. "R_max = 43.5 × (V − 10.5)" equals 1 / 23 mA).
