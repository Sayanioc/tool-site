What this calculator does

It converts a transmitter's analogue output between milliamps, percent of span and the process value in engineering units, for any lower and upper range value (LRV/URV) – including suppressed, elevated and reversed ranges. In square-root mode it handles DP flow meters (orifice, venturi, V-cone) where the transmitter output is linear in differential pressure and flow is proportional to √DP.

Formula

% of span  = (mA − 4) / 16 × 100
mA         = 4 + 16 × % / 100
Linear:      PV = LRV + (URV − LRV) × % / 100
Square root: PV = LRV + (URV − LRV) × √(% / 100)     (% = DP signal)
             DP % = (flow %)² / 100

Worked example

A pressure transmitter is ranged 0–10 bar and reads 8 mA. Percent = (8 − 4)/16 × 100 = 25 %. PV = 0 + 10 × 0.25 = 2.5 bar.

A DP flow transmitter (linear output, square root in the DCS) is ranged for 0–200 m³/h and reads 12 mA. DP = 50 %, flow = 200 × √0.5 = 141.42 m³/h (70.71 % flow). Half flow (50 %) is only 25 % DP = 8 mA.

Common mistakes

  • Double square root: extracting the root in both the transmitter and the DCS. Check where extraction happens (transmitter transfer function vs DCS block) – it must be done once.
  • Using 0–20 instead of 4–20: 12 mA is 50 %, not 60 %.
  • Forgetting low-flow cut-off in square-root mode: near zero DP, small signal noise gives large flow errors (1 % DP = 10 % flow). Most systems cut off below 5–10 % flow.
  • Reading 3.6 mA or 21 mA as a process value – under NAMUR NE 43 these are failure signals, not measurements.
  • Wrong sign on elevated or suppressed ranges (e.g. wet-leg level LRV is negative).

Standards and references

  • IEC 60381-1 – analogue signals for process control systems: 4–20 mA direct current.
  • NAMUR NE 43 – standardisation of the signal level for failure information of digital transmitters (3.8–20.5 mA measuring range, ≤ 3.6 / ≥ 21 mA failure).
  • ISO 5167 – flow measurement by differential pressure devices (flow ∝ √ΔP).
